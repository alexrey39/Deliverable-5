2. Summary

I looked at reported crime in Los Angeles from 2020 to 2024 using a dataset with 1,004,894 crime reports and 28 original variables. I wanted to see how the number of crime reports changed each year, what types of crimes were most common, and which police areas had the most reports. I used R to count reports by year, crime description, and area. I also made graphs for victim age, area totals, the relationship between age and crime time, and crime types over time.

The highest number of reports was in 2022 with 235,259. The most common crime description was vehicle theft, with 115,184 reports, and Central had the highest number of reports among LAPD areas, with 69,668. The number for 2024 was lower than earlier years, but I have to be careful when explaining that difference because the LAPD changed its reporting system in 2024. These numbers describe the records in this dataset, not every crime that happened in Los Angeles.

3. Purpose

My goal is to understand patterns in reported crime in Los Angeles between 2020 and 2024. My main questions are:

  1. How many crimes were reported for each year, and did the number increase or decrease?
  2. What were the most common types of crimes?
  3. Which LAPD areas had the most crime reports?

I also looked at victim age and crime time to explore other patterns in the data.

Cleaning and preparing the data

The data was already tidy enough for my questions because one row is one crime report. I did not need to use pivot_longer() or pivot_wider(). I made a Year column using the first four characters of DATE OCC, and an Hour column using TIME OCC. Then I filtered the file to 2020 through 2024. I kept the same columns instead of reshaping the whole dataset.

crime <- crime_raw
crime$Year <- as.numeric(substr(crime$`DATE OCC`, 1, 4))
crime$Hour <- floor(as.numeric(crime$`TIME OCC`) / 100)

crime <- crime %>%
  filter(Year >= 2020, Year <= 2024)

6. Results

I used count() to group reports by year, crime description, and LAPD area. I used summary() for victim ages. These methods fit my project because I am mainly trying to compare how often crimes appear in different years and categories. I did not need a complicated model to answer these questions.

Question 1: Did reported crime increase or decrease? The data went from 199,847 reports in 2020 to 235,259 in 2022, then 232,345 in 2023 and 127,567 in 2024. That means the count increased between 2020 and 2022, then decreased in the remaining years. The yearly table and line graph show this pattern. Because the reporting system changed in 2024, I cannot say that the full drop represents a real decrease in crime.

Question 2: What crimes were most common? Vehicle theft had 115,184 reports, simple assault had 74,821, and burglary from a vehicle had 63,515. The crime frequency table supports this answer.

Question 3: Which areas reported the most crime? Central had 69,668 reports, followed by 77th Street with 61,756 and Pacific with 59,513. The area table and bar graph show this comparison. These are totals, not population-adjusted crime rates.

7. Conclusions

This project helped me understand how crime reports in Los Angeles changed over the five years and which crimes and areas appeared most often. I found the most reports in 2022, vehicle theft was the most common description, and Central had the highest area total. The graphs also showed patterns in victim age, although the scatter plot did not show a clear relationship between age and hour of crime.

The biggest limitation is that the dataset only includes crimes recorded by the LAPD. Some columns have missing information, and the reporting system changed in March 2024. The area counts also do not account for population. If I continued the project, I would compare crime types by month and look more closely at what changed in 2024. I could also use population data to compare areas more fairly.
